What the three classes mean

VERIFIED means the behaviour was observed on a running Paper 26.2 server — a log line, a database row, a file on disk after shutdown. TESTED means the logic beneath it is covered by the automated suite against a real SQLite file, not a mock. OPEN means it is built and audited but nobody has watched it work.

One caveat applies to the whole ledger and is worth stating once: this build environment has no Minecraft client, so no in-game GUI has been opened by a person. Where an item is marked TESTED, that describes the service logic and the server-side behaviour beneath the menus — the money, the stock, the persistence — and not the experience of clicking through them. The menu framework itself is covered structurally: MenuManager cancels every click before a handler runs, refuses every item-moving click type, and logs a click on an unregistered slot as a security violation.

What still needs a human

Four requirements cannot be closed from a headless build. None are expected to be broken; all four are the difference between built to the constraint and seen working.

  • GUIs work on Java.

    To close: open each of the 27 menus on a real client and buy something. The framework is audited and the services behind it are tested; the click path is not.

  • GUIs work through Geyser where technically possible.

    To close: join from a Bedrock device through Geyser and walk the six checks in docs/CROSSPLAY.md — menu rendering at each size, the chat-prompt flow with a touch keyboard, confirmation tap accuracy, and whether particle visualisation is visible enough to be useful at all.

  • Claim visualization works.

    To close: run /claim visualize and look. Because particles are unreliable through Geyser and easy to lose against a bright sky on a phone, visualising also prints the corner coordinates and size to chat — the reliable channel on every platform. Neither half has been seen.

  • Admin GUI works.

    To close: open /servercore admin. Partly mitigated: the same information is available as text through /servercore status, which is verified live.

The ledger

Economy and trade 6 items

Tested Economy works.

Money is a long of minor units throughout, never a double. 21 cases in EconomyServiceTest, including "a transfer fee is destroyed, not given to anyone" and "the balance ceiling is enforced and rolls the whole payment back". Three flows stay distinct per §52: creation, sinks, and transfers.

Tested /pay works.

Registered as pay. "a payment moves exactly the stated amount", "paying more than you have changes nothing", "a refused payment writes nothing to the ledger". The fee is included in the affordability check rather than charged after it.

Tested /balance works.

Registered with aliases bal and money. "an unknown account reads as zero rather than failing", "history shows both sides of a payment", "ranking orders by balance". There is deliberately no balance cache to go stale.

Tested Server shop works.

Catalogue load confirmed live at boot: Server shop loaded: 12 categories, 96 items. ShopLogicTest refuses the two ways a catalogue can leak money — "sell price above buy price is a money loop" and "an item with neither price cannot be traded" — at load rather than at purchase.

Tested Auction House works.

18 cases. Full item fidelity via serializeAsBytes, so enchantments and custom names survive. "concurrent buyers: exactly one wins the listing", "cancel and buy cannot both succeed on one listing", "purging never removes anything still owed to somebody". A collection box removes the refund path entirely, so there is no state where money and item are both in flight.

Tested Player shops work.

Index rebuilt live at boot: Loaded 1 player shop(s). 12 cases, including "concurrent buyers cannot take more stock than exists", "stock cannot be taken below zero" and "updating prices leaves stock alone".

Land and tenancy 7 items

Tested Land claims work.

42 cases across ClaimServiceTest and ClaimIndexTest: "land and money move together or not at all", "claims survive a restart, including members and flags", "expanding charges only for the blocks added". Overlap is re-checked inside the write transaction, which is what closed a race where eight threads all claimed the same land.

Open Claim visualization works.

Particle boundary plus a chat fallback printing corner coordinates and size, because particles fade fast and read poorly on a phone. Built, never seen. See What still needs a human.

Verified Premium spawn plots work.

Exercised live this session: a plot seeded into sc_plot was picked up by the sweep and transitioned correctly. Backed by 15 cases including "overlapping plots cannot be created" and "concurrent purchases of one plot cannot both succeed".

Tested Plot purchases cost money.

"buying a plot charges the price and sets the first rent a period out" — not immediately, so a buyer does not pay twice on day one. "a plot that cannot be afforded is neither charged for nor transferred".

Verified Plot rent works.

Observed live: a plot with rent_due_at 24 hours in the past moved OWNED → RENT_OVERDUE when /plot sweep ran. Tests add "rent is collected when it falls due" and "the sweep does not charge twice for one period". Next-due is period-aligned in O(1), so a server offline for a month does not bill a tenant thirty times.

Verified Rent grace periods work.

The same live sweep wrote grace_ends_at exactly 48 hours out, matching the configured grace. "a failed payment starts a grace period and leaves shops trading" and "paying during the grace period restores the plot and reopens shops". A grace period at least as long as the rent period is refused at config load, since it would mean rent could never fall overdue.

Verified Expired shops are handled safely.

Per §27 stock is never silently deleted. Verified live: an expiring plot serialised its shop stock into the recovery mailbox as a 257-byte entry rather than dropping it. "mailbox entries can only be collected once". Serialisation is wrapped so that a failure to preserve stock cannot abort the expiry itself.

Players and progression 9 items

Tested TPA works.

Five commands registered — tpa, tpahere, tpaccept, tpdeny, tpacancel — from one class, because duplicating the handling is how /tpahere ends up teleporting the wrong player. "traveller and destination are never the same player", "a request expires at its deadline, not after it". Warm-up compares against the origin rather than the previous tick, so slow drifting cannot creep past the check.

Tested Kills persist.

"statistics survive closing and reopening the database". Anti-farm is windowed per pair: "the anti-farm window is per killer-victim pair" and "kills outside the window no longer count against it".

Tested Deaths persist.

Same repository and persistence guarantee. "concurrent increments do not lose counts", and "kill/death ratio treats zero deaths as the kill count" rather than dividing by zero.

Tested Playtime persists.

Flushed periodically and on quit, so a crash costs at most one interval rather than a whole session. "several counters can be written together".

Tested Money leaderboards work.

The one board not backed by a counter — it reads account balances directly. "money is the one statistic not backed by a counter", "money is abbreviated so a sign stays readable".

Tested Kill leaderboards work.

LeaderboardStat.KILLS over the statistics repository. "leaderboards order by value and exclude zeroes", so a board is never padded with players who have done nothing.

Tested Death leaderboards work.

LeaderboardStat.DEATHS, same ranking framework. "counts are grouped with thousands separators".

Tested Playtime leaderboards work.

"playtime renders as a duration, not a second count", and "every statistic has a default title and a format" — so a new statistic cannot be added without deciding how it reads.

Verified Physical spawn leaderboards work.

Four TextDisplay boards spawned and counted on a running server. Each is marked non-persistent and recreated from the database at boot, so a removed board cannot orphan an entity in the world. Player names and operator-supplied titles are both escaped: "an operator-supplied title cannot inject a click action".

Platform and operations 6 items

Open GUIs work on Java.

27 menus built against a framework that refreshes in place rather than reopening, and treats every click as hostile until matched to a registered slot. Never opened by a person. See What still needs a human.

Open GUIs work through Geyser where technically possible.

The Phase 12 audit swept every menu for interactions a touch device cannot produce. Four were found and three were real defects: Bedrock shop owners could stock a shop but never reprice it or withdraw anything; claim owners could add a trusted player but never remove one; and clearing a search was right-click only, stranding a player on an empty page. All three now have labelled plain-click buttons. No Bedrock device was available to confirm.

Verified Permissions work.

A 22-node server.* tree rooted at server.admin; /servercore status reports the resolved provider live. A live-only defect was caught here: the server.shop.category.* nodes were undeclared, and Bukkit defaults an unknown permission to op-only — so non-op players saw an empty shop. Fixed by registering them at runtime with PermissionDefault.TRUE. No unit test would have found that.

Open Admin GUI works.

Gathers live server state off the main thread. Not opened by a person — though the same figures are reachable as text through /servercore status, which is verified. See What still needs a human.

Verified Configuration works.

Boot merged new defaults into both files without clobbering existing values — Added new default keys to config.yml — and /servercore reload ran over RCON, writing an audit row naming the actor. No gameplay value is hard-coded; prices, limits, periods and cooldowns all come from config.

Verified Data survives restarts.

Tested destructively. The server was hard-killed mid-run; on restart SQLite recovered the write-ahead log and the migrator correctly reported the schema already at head rather than re-running it. A graceful shutdown folded a 119 KB WAL and 32 KB shared-memory file into a single 266 KB database, removing both.

Engineering guarantees 5 items

Tested Transactions are protected against duplication.

Every balance change is a conditional update — SET balance = balance - ? WHERE uuid = ? AND balance >= ? — whose success is the affected row count, so a double spend cannot commit even if two threads read the same balance. The same shape guards stock and listings: "a sold listing cannot be bought again", "an item can only be collected once".

Tested Concurrent operations are safe.

Five races run with real threads against a real SQLite file, not a mock: payments out of one account, buyers of one auction listing, buyers of one shop's stock, claims on the same land, and counter increments. Each asserts exactly one winner and that the losers left the money supply unchanged — "concurrent payments never create or destroy money". SQLite runs in IMMEDIATE transaction mode so writers collide at begin rather than at commit.

Tested Critical exploits have been tested.

Attacked on purpose rather than assumed prevented. 20 cases in ExploitResistanceTest covering the §50 list: "a negative payment cannot be used to steal", "a negative withdrawal cannot be used to mint money", "a quantity that would overflow a total is refused, not wrapped", "a manager cannot promote an accomplice to manager". A further 5 cases fire 16 hostile MiniMessage payloads at the escaper and assert on the rendered component — no click event, no hover event, text intact — rather than on the escaped string, which is MiniMessage's business rather than ours.

Tested Performance is acceptable.

Measured rather than asserted — see The number that mattered below.

Verified Documentation exists.

A README plus three reference documents in the repository — ARCHITECTURE.md, CROSSPLAY.md and ECONOMY.md. Every figure on this page is cited from them or from the test suite.

The number that mattered

One lookup on this plugin's hot path runs on the main thread for every block break, place and interaction on the server. Its cost is multiplied by player activity rather than player count, which makes it the one piece worth measuring at a size no test server will reach.

Workload against ClaimIndexResult
200,000 lookups across a 50,000-claim index219 ms · ~1.1 µs each
200,000 lookups in a world with no claims5 ms
Headroom in one 50 ms server tick~45,000 lookups

Claims are bucketed by chunk, so a lookup is two hash probes and a scan bounded by one chunk. A linear scan over the same index would need minutes. The regression test holds this to a budget two orders of magnitude looser than the measurement, so it fails on genuine algorithmic regression rather than on a slow machine — and it also hammers the index from four reader threads while a fifth writes, because that is exactly how the real index is used: written by the database thread, read by the main thread.

Limitations documented, not worked around

Specification §66 asks for platform limits to be identified and designed around rather than papered over with something fragile. Two were found worth recording.

Asynchronous command replies never reach an RCON sender

Every command that touches the database computes off the main thread and delivers its reply through thenSync. RCON is request/response: Paper closes the response window when the command method returns, which is before the asynchronous work finishes.

The work itself still runs — confirmed by seeding an overdue plot, sweeping over RCON, and watching the row change — but anything sent to the sender afterwards is discarded. The console and in-game players are unaffected; both senders outlive the command.

Why it was not "fixed": the only way to land the reply inside the response window is to block the main thread until the query returns, trading a cosmetic limitation for a server-wide stall. Reliability over feature count — so it is documented, and administrators who need to read an answer should use the console or an in-game account.

Bedrock text entry does not round-trip, so no menu uses it

Anvil renaming and sign editing are the two usual ways a Java plugin takes text from a player. Neither survives Geyser reliably — Bedrock opens a native sign dialog the server sees differently. Rather than detect the platform and branch, every menu that needs text uses a chat prompt, which behaves identically on both platforms. The cost is one extra step for Java players; the benefit is one code path instead of two, and no platform-specific breakage.
